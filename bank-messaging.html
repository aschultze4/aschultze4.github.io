Bank Messaging

Rebuilding trust in fraud and scam alerts across platforms.

Lead researcher · 10 weeks · Qualitative + quantitative research · Cross-platform alert messaging

Context

Fraud and scams cost people billions annually. With 30+ fraud communication types, it was time to standardize our approach in order to increase message credibility and customer engagement.

Business Goal

Increase digital customer engagement to lower call volume and prevent fraud and scam losses.

Research Goal

Understand what drives customer trust in our messages.

Impact

The team will pursue a partnership to improve sender verification.

The strategy I defined around consistent branding, scam education, and providing multiple secure paths with reassurance is already being implemented.

Constraints

  • Limited time and budget for project scope
  • Could not run live A/B experiments which impacted situational accuracy
Alignment workshop: Defining four message dimensions and a menu of study options

Approach

After stakeholder interviews, I pitched a new research approach to identify what actually drives trust. We aligned on:

Click test: Test two design versions using a 30s time limit with 600 digitally active users across varying scam awareness levels to help us understand how threat perception affects message interpretation.

Lean think-aloud study: Foundational behavioral research with an equal split of 70 mobile and web users with mixed scam experience to uncover patterns across platforms.

Key Findings

Click test results revealed critical insights about behavior under pressure

Button design didn't affect accuracy (50% vs. 51.8%) or speed across versions, enabling a confident design decision.

However, the low accuracy and 21-27% of user clicks on incorrect CTAs formed a behavioral hypothesis: Users struggle to identify their expected action in urgent situations.

Trust is the foundation of message performance

I identified key trust signals that influence whether users engage with messages. One signal, educational scam warnings, proved unexpectedly powerful and easy to implement.

One participant captured this perfectly:

A scammer wouldn't tell you that.

I discovered which messages warrant additional context by outlining situations where customers are less likely to trust or act on a message.

Outcomes

I delivered a comprehensive design strategy for scam and fraud messaging with specific recommendations across copy, visual design, message timing, and verification paths. The team is currently implementing recommendations which are projected to reduce call volume and prevent customer losses.

The team initiated a sender verification partnership that was informed by the findings on a specific messaging channel.

Based on the click test findings, the design team moved forward with brand colors for button styles.

Reflection

If I were to do this project again, I would set out to define the dimensions of a successful communication earlier.
