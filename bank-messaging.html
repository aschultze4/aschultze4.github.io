Bank Messaging

Rebuilding trust in fraud and scam alerts across platforms.

Lead researcher · 10 weeks · Qualitative + quantitative research · Cross-platform alert messaging

Context

Fraud and scams cost people billions annually. With 30+ fraud communication types, it was time to standardize our approach in order to increase message credibility and customer engagement.

Business Goal

Increase digital customer engagement to lower call volume and prevent fraud + scam losses.

Research Goal

Understand what drives customer trust in our messages.

Constraints

  • Limited time and budget for project scope
  • Could not run live A/B experiments which impacted situational accuracy

Approach

After stakeholder interviews, I pitched a new research approach to identify what actually drives trust. We aligned on:

Click test: Test two design versions under a 30-second time limit to determine how button design affected accuracy and understanding.

  • User segment: 600 digitally active users with varying scam awareness levels to capture perception variance.

Lean think-aloud study: Foundational behavioral insights that accommodated a low budget and quick turnaround.

  • User segment: 70 customers split by platform preference (mobile/web) and scam experience to identify platform-specific patterns.
Alignment workshop: defining four message dimensions and study menu options

Key Findings

Click test results revealed critical insights about user behavior under pressure

Button design alone did not meaningfully affect click accuracy, which remained low across both versions (50% vs. 51.8%). Time to click and perception ratings were similarly consistent (12s vs. 13s). This allowed the team to confidently make a design decision aligned with brand standards.

However, the overall low accuracy and high rate of clicks on incorrect CTAs (21-27%) formed an early hypothesis: users struggle to identify their expected action in urgent situations. This theory was confirmed in subsequent qualitative research.

Trust is the foundation of message performance

Customers automatically categorize scam-related communications as urgent, meaning we can scale back language to improve message legitimacy. I identified key trust signals that influence whether users engage with messages, including sender consistency, recognizable transaction details, and clear consequences. One signal (scam warnings and education) proved unexpectedly powerful and easy to implement.

In the words of a participant:

A scammer wouldn't tell you that.

I also discovered specific situations where customers are less likely to trust or act on a message, informing the team which messages warrant additional context and which are less sensitive.

Outcomes

This is an early-stage project likely to undergo further evaluative research. Looking forward, my proposed strategy centers around consistent branding, enhanced consequence + transaction details, and providing multiple secure paths. Thanks to the results on a specific type of message, the team will pursue a partnership to improve sender verification.

Reflection

If I were to do this project again, I would set out to define the dimensions of a successful communication earlier. I would also create a map of the communication types to make it more clear what design changes were feasible.
