Feature Upgrade

Mapping customer mental models for a digital feature upgrade.

Lead researcher · 12 weeks · Mixed methods

Context

The company was planning to upgrade a specific feature (Feature D) that allowed customers to complete Task X.

Task X had always required paperwork, but the company had just taken on a vendor to provide a digital-first experience.

We had seen some initial findings on security perceptions, but we needed to understand customer mental models around Task X.

Research Goal: Conduct a full research effort, from initial generative work to MVP usability testing, in order to successfully upgrade a feature.

Constraints

  • Our company was unable to alter a few screens from the vendor templates.
  • The team uncovered a business risk in one part of the flow that rendered our prototype out of date during testing.

Approach

  • Initial background: I relied on past portfolio research and conducted a competitive scrape.
  • Navigation and findability: I chose to conduct a tree test and click test to validate new and existing entry points.
  • Usability: I ran a comprehension survey to ensure we were headed in the right direction, using those findings to inform in-depth usability interviews.

Key Findings

  • Customers do not naturally assume that they can complete this service with us digitally. This finding had implications for entry points and marketing.
  • Convenience was typically valued over security when it came to this feature, but I discovered one point in the flow where security reassurance really made a difference.
  • The part of the experience that incurred business risk was also less understandable to users. We recommended a specific approach based on how customers typically thought about the process.

Outcomes

Our findings formed a concrete idea of how customers understand and use this feature. This helped the design and product teams to discuss strategy with the vendor and update feature implementation.

The designs were updated to meet my recommendations for security reassurance and human-centric language. The entry points validated by testing were approved.

Reflection

The product is up for launch shortly, and I plan to advocate for research tracking.

This was the first external vendor experience I worked on, and it taught me how to navigate complex partnerships to advocate for the user.
