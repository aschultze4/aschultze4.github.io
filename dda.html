Feature Upgrade

Mapping customer mental models for a digital feature upgrade.

Lead researcher · 12 weeks · Mixed methods · Mobile app + online banking

Context

The company has a feature (Feature D) that enables customers to complete Task X. Task X had always required paperwork, but the company had just partnered with a vendor to provide a digital-first experience.

Business Goal

Determine whether the vendor's digital solution aligns with customer expectations for Task X.

Research Goal

Uncover customer mental models for completing Task X to identify gaps between user expectations and the proposed vendor designs.

Constraints

  • Our company was unable to alter some screens from the vendor templates.
  • The team uncovered a business risk in one part of the flow that rendered our prototype out of date during testing.

Approach

  • Secondary and competitive research: I pulled together past portfolio data and conducted a competitor scrape to formulate a starting point for further work.
  • Card sort: I chose to conduct a card sort to validate new and existing entry points.
  • Comprehension survey: Surveying 150 participants on security and Task Xmental models before presenting early stage concepts to assess understanding.
  • Click test: Measuring findability and navigation for proposed entry points with 300 users, controlled for screen length.
  • Usability testing: In-depth interviews where participants answered questions about concepts and interacted with a prototype.

Key Findings

Task X exists outside of our platforms

Customers do not naturally assume that they can complete Task X with us digitally. Though the click test results for the entry points were fairly high around 83% and 74% accuracy, the comprehension survey showed that 66% associate Task X with a passive 'receiving' role instead of the design of Feature D.

Qualitative findings showed an overwhelming association with this role that would impact whether customers are even looking for digital entry points. This insight had significant implications for the marketing and entry points of Feature D.

Convenience over security (most of the time)

Convenience was typically valued over security specifically for Feature D. But the survey and usability testing uncovered one point in the flow where security reassurance really made a difference.

Customers don't know what that is!

The area of the flow that incurred the business risk also used a term that was less understandable to users, uncovered early in the research and validated by usability testing.

I would not have any idea how to access a ******* ******. (comprehension survey participant)
I just know we do it through *******, I don't know about the actual ******* ******. (usability test participant)

I recommended an educational design approach based on how customers typically thought about Task X.

Outcomes

Our findings formed a concrete picture of how customers understand Task X compared to how they use Feature D. This helped the design and product teams to discuss strategy with the vendor and update feature implementation.

The designs for Feature D were updated based on my recommendations for security reassurance at a specific screen, and human-centric language and education in the area of the flow that mitigated business risk.

The entry points validated by testing were approved, and my work led to more support for marketing the updated feature.

Reflection

Feature D is in production for launch in Q1 2026.

This was the first external vendor experience I worked on, and it taught me how to navigate complex partnerships to advocate for our customers.
