Feature Upgrade

Mapping customer mental models for a digital feature upgrade.

Lead researcher · 12 weeks · Mixed methods · Mobile app + online banking

Context

The company was planning to upgrade a specific feature (Feature D) that allowed customers to complete Task X.

Task X had always required paperwork, but the company had just taken on a vendor to provide a digital-first experience.

We had some past foundational findings on security perceptions, but we needed to understand customer mental models around Task X.

Research Goal: Conduct a full research effort, from initial generative work to MVP usability testing, in order to upgrade a feature.

Constraints

  • Our company was unable to alter a few screens from the vendor templates.
  • The team uncovered a business risk in one part of the flow that rendered our prototype out of date during testing.

Approach

  • Initial background: I pulled together past portfolio research and conducted a competitive scrape.
  • Navigation and findability: I chose to conduct a tree test and click test to validate new and existing entry points.
  • Usability: I ran a comprehension survey to ensure we were headed in the right direction, and used those findings to guide in-depth usability interviews.

Key Findings

  • Customers do not naturally assume that they can complete Task X with us digitally. This finding had implications for the entry points and marketing of Feature D.
  • Convenience was typically valued over security when it came to Feature D, but I discovered one point in the flow where security reassurance really made a difference.
  • The area of the flow that incurred business risk was also less understandable to users. We recommended a specific educational approach based on how customers typically thought about Task X.

Outcomes

Our findings formed a concrete picture of how customers understand Task X compared to how they use Feature D. This helped the design and product teams to discuss strategy with the vendor and update feature implementation.

The designs for Feature D were updated to meet my recommendations for security reassurance and human-centric language. The entry points validated by testing were approved.

Reflection

Feature D is rolling out Q1 2026, and I plan to advocate for research tracking.

This was the first external vendor experience I worked on, and it taught me how to navigate complex partnerships to advocate for our customers.
