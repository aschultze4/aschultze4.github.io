Balance Assist Loans

Improving short-term loan applications for at-risk populations.

Co-lead researcher · 5 weeks · Qualitative research · Mobile app

Context

Balance Assist offers a $500 loan for established checking customers with only a $5 fee. The team wanted to evaluate the usability of the Balance Assist loan application, and iterate on designs.

Beyond improving the tool for vulnerable customers who need cash quickly, the research team set out to make sure people are aware of the terms and capabilities.

Research Goal: Outline potential usability issues with a short-term loan application, prioritizing for impact on the projected loan applicant pool.

Constraints

  • There are more legal language restrictions around loan designs than almost any other product.
  • Partners were pushing for a dark pattern that would increase autopay signups.
  • There were technical limitations around the calculator aspect of the tool.

Approach

  • Since quantitative foundational research was already in place, I conducted qualitative usability sessions with a prototype to measure task completion and understanding.
  • I recruited customers who had a higher borrowing tendency around emergency expenses, with careful attention to possible overreach during research.

Key Findings

Users were able to successfully complete the application. I prioritized several blockers:

  • Some customers were unclear on what kind of credit check was required. From a behavioral perspective, it was vital that this information was communicated early and often.
Okay, it says you're going to check my credit. But is it a hard or soft inquiry?
  • A link to payment breakdowns was noticed by fewer than half of participants, and some customers assumed that there had to be further fees beyond the $5 listed.
  • As expected, the dark pattern in the autopay dialogue caused participants to make mistakes.

All of the findings impacted customers' understanding of the loan and repayment.

Outcomes

  • I made it clear that we needed to prioritize the hard credit check requirement in our designs, while also keeping customers aware that we account for the client relationship.
  • I advocated for clear and expanded information on the fee in order to make customers more confident about application and repayment.
  • I recommended new approaches for the repayment breakdown link and autopay signup based on why participants didn't see or understand each component.

The product and design teams took the recommendations and made application improvements.

Reflection

Though recommendations were not always implemented the way I expected, the application did benefit from my findings.

This was one of the first times I worked with participants experiencing financial difficulty and stressors, and it taught me how to navigate conversations and expectations in a new way.
