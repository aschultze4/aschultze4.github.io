Balance Assist Loans

Improving short-term loan applications for vulnerable populations.

Co-lead researcher · 5 weeks · Qualitative research · Mobile app

Context

Balance Assist offers a $500 loan for established checking customers with only a $5 fee. The team wanted to evaluate the usability of the Balance Assist loan application, and iterate on designs.

Beyond improving the tool for at-risk customers who need cash quickly, the research team set out to make sure people are aware of the terms and capabilities.

Research Goal

Outline potential usability issues with a short-term loan application, prioritizing for impact on the projected loan applicant pool.

Constraints

  • There are more legal language restrictions around loan designs than almost any other product.
  • Partners were pushing for a dark pattern that would increase autopay signups.
  • Prospective users were difficult and risky to recruit

Approach

  • Foundational research: Another researcher had conducted exploratory research. I worked with her to translate findings into a hypothesis on how customers might interact with the application.
  • Recruitment concerns: I recruited customers who had a higher borrowing tendency around emergency expenses, avoiding possible overreach during recruitment and research.
  • Usability testing: I conducted interview sessions with a prototype to measure task completion and understanding.

Key Findings

Users successfully completed the application, but I identified several critical blockers that impacted customers' understanding of the loan and repayment terms.

Credit check confusion created unacceptable friction

Some customers were unclear on what kind of credit check was required. From a risk perspective, communicating this information early and often would prevent drop-off.

Okay, it says you're going to check my credit. But is it a hard or soft inquiry?

Payment transparency issues undermined trust

A link to payment breakdowns was noticed by fewer than half of participants, and some customers assumed that there had to be further fees beyond the $5 listed.

This lack of clarity reduced confidence in the loan terms.

Dark pattern in autopay dialogue caused mistakes

As I predicted, the autopay dialogue's design led participants to make unintended selections, potentially enrolling them in a plan they didn't want.

Outcomes

I made it clear that we needed to prioritize the hard credit check requirement in our designs, while keeping customers aware that we account for their relationship with us.

  • The team expanded and moved this information to the splash page before entering the application.

I advocated for clear and expanded information on the fee in order to make at-risk customers more confident about application and repayment.

  • The team simplified the copy that explained the fee, and added my proposed simple question "Is $5 the only fee?" to the FAQ before entering the application.

I recommended new approaches for the repayment breakdown link and autopay signup based on why participants didn't see or understand each component.

  • The link was repositioned and increased in size.
  • My work on the autopay signup initiated a conversation with our brand strategy team, leading to a design system update for message box CTAs.

Reflection

Projects and permissions move fast, and I don't have visibility into whether the dark pattern preferred by PMs was removed. However, it was likely updated, based on my work that reinforced how we treat message box CTAs.

This was one of the first times I worked with participants experiencing financial difficulty and stressors, and it taught me how to navigate conversations and expectations in a new way.
