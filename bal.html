Balance Assist Loans

Improving short-term loan applications for vulnerable populations.

Co-lead researcher · 5 weeks · Qualitative moderated research · Mobile app

Context

Balance Assist offers a $500 loan for established checking customers with only a $5 fee. The team wanted to evaluate the usability of the Balance Assist loan application, and discover awareness of the loan terms.

Business Goal

Increase application completion rates and autopay signups while ensuring vulnerable customers fully understand the loan terms.

Research Goal

Outline potential usability issues with a short-term loan application, prioritizing for impact on the projected loan applicant pool.

Impact

My research led to:

  • Reduced application drop-off
  • Increased credit transparency for at-risk customers
  • Updated design system standard around proposed dark pattern

Constraints

  • There are more legal restrictions around loan language and designs than almost any other product.
  • Partners were pushing for a dark pattern that would increase autopay signups.
  • Prospective users represented a challenging and risky recruit.

Approach

  • Foundational research: Another researcher had conducted exploratory research. I worked with her to translate findings into a hypothesis on how customers might interact with the application.
  • Recruitment concerns: I recruited 10 customers who had a higher borrowing tendency around emergency expenses and avoided overreach during recruitment and research.
  • Usability testing: I conducted interview sessions with a prototype to measure task completion and understanding.

Key Findings

Users successfully completed the application, but I identified several critical blockers that impacted customers' understanding of the loan and repayment terms.

Credit check confusion created unacceptable friction

Some customers were unclear on what kind of credit check was required. Communicating this information early and often would decrease risk and prevent drop-off.

Okay, it says you're going to check my credit. But is it a hard or soft inquiry?

Payment transparency issues undermined trust

A link to payment breakdowns was noticed by fewer than half of participants, and some customers assumed that there had to be further fees beyond the $5 listed.

This lack of clarity reduced confidence in the loan terms.

Dark pattern in autopay dialogue caused mistakes

As I predicted, the autopay dialogue's design led participants to make unintended selections, potentially enrolling them in a plan they didn't want.

Outcomes

Improved credit check transparency

I identified that customers needed more clarity about the credit check to build trust and prevent drop-off. The team moved this information to the splash page before application entry, clearly communicating the requirement and how we factor in existing customer relationships.

Clear fee details to build confidence

I advocated for clearer communication around the $5 fee to address customer skepticism. The team simplified the fee explanation and added my proposed FAQ question "Is $5 the only fee?" before application entry, directly addressing the most common concern.

Enhanced visibility of key information

My analysis of the autopay signup flow sparked a broader conversation with our brand strategy team, ultimately leading to a design system update for how we handle CTAs across products.

I recommended design improvements for the repayment breakdown link based on user behavior patterns. The team repositioned and enlarged the link for better discoverability.

Reflection

This project was an important learning opportunity around balancing business pressures with user advocacy in financial products. Working with customers experiencing financial stress was a formative experience that contributed to my ability to conduct sensitive research with vulnerable populations.
